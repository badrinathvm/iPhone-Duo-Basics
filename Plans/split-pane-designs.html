Six ways to show Primary and Secondary

Today the panes are two gradient cards with an icon and a name, and the ratio slider lives inside the primary one. Below are six directions that say more about what a split arrangement is. Change Axes or Ratio and every mockup responds, just as .arrangementViewStyle(.split.axes(…)) and .splitArrangementLayoutRatio(…) do.

What I'd fix in the current design

  1. The ratio slider sits inside the primary pane. The pane resizes while you drag the slider inside it, so the control slides away from your finger. Move it next to the Axes picker in the bottom glass bar, or onto the seam (D).
  2. axis: nil tells the reader nothing. The chip code (axisDescription) is commented out in the current file, so the screenshot is from an earlier build. SplitArrangementView already holds axes; pass it down, or the real environment value if the SDK exposes one.
  3. The panes are only an icon and a name. A hint of role content (a list vs. a detail) makes "primary" and "secondary" mean something.
  4. Keep the ViewThatFits icon fallback. It is the right idea. The mockups above copy it: shrink a pane and the icon and extras drop away.
  5. Small: the catalog row title reads "Split Arrangments" in Utils/DuoSections.swift.

Recommendation

A for the panes, plus D's seam handle for the ratio. It stays close to what you have, explains the two roles, and removes the awkward in-pane slider. Fix the chip to show the real axis.

  • B is a good fit if this sample doubles as API documentation.
  • E is worth borrowing from if you want the sample to show axis-aware content.
  • C is the richest but is a mini-app; F is polish you can layer on last.

Assumptions to confirm

  • splitArrangementLayoutRatio is the primary pane's share of the split, clamped to 0.2–0.8 as in your slider. The mockups place the seam where the ratio puts it. The physical hinge position isn't modelled.
  • Horizontal axes mean side by side, and vertical means stacked, as in your screenshot.
  • Chips that show the axis assume the pane can be given axes. I don't know what the SDK exposes to a pane, and the screenshot's axis: nil suggests it may be nil.
  • These are HTML/CSS design targets, so SwiftUI rendering will differ in detail.
